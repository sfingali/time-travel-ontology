# The Endless (2017)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Astronomy Trek and fan charts map Camp Arcadia’s nested “time pods” by duration (seconds to ~10 years).

## Short blurb
Brothers Justin and Aaron return to Camp Arcadia, the UFO death cult they fled a decade earlier, and find the region partitioned into invisible time loops run by an unseen Entity that kills and resets inmates for sport—or worship.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

A VHS from Camp Arcadia claims “ascension.” Aaron remembers a warm commune; Justin remembers a suicide cult. Stuck in dead-end jobs, they visit for one day. Members look unaged. Hal’s equations, twin moons, a lake buoy photo, and locked tape libraries hint at something wrong.

Justin explores the perimeter: wooden markers bounce travelers back; a man locked in a seconds-long scream loop; **Chris and Mike** from Benson/Moorhead’s prior film *Resolution* still trapped in a roughly week-long pod. “Shitty Carl” endures a ~3-hour trailer hell. Inhabitants say a third moon means permanent trapping; suicide only restarts the pod.

Hal admits ignorance dressed as math and urges Justin to read the Entity’s clues. Monoliths depict cultural names for the same watcher. Aaron wants to stay—immortality with people who care beats menial loneliness—even if “ascension” is sacred death-by-Entity each decade-long Arcadia cycle. Justin confesses he wrongfully dominated Aaron’s life; Aaron, finally heard, agrees to leave. They jump-start the car and barely flee as the Entity manifests and the camp resets; remaining members look wistful.

Directors leave open whether the brothers inhabit a still-larger unnoticed loop; interpersonal submission patterns are framed as their private endless cycle.

## Paradox analysis

- **Not traveler TT:** Victims don’t choose destinations; space is partitioned into prisons.
- **Death = restart:** Like a horror Groundhog, but geographically locked.
- **Entity as observer-god:** Feeds on hope/fear; cult reframes predation as ascension.
- **Skill accumulation:** Long loops explain magic tricks and advanced math—time as XP.
- **Shared universe:** *Resolution* characters still looping—franchise ontology.
- **Free will theme:** Escape requires breaking interpersonal domination loops, not just driving.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Well Go USA; shot as microbudget passion project by Benson/Moorhead. Critical cult favorite. Ties to *Resolution* (2012) and later *Something in the Dirt*. Runtime ~111 min.

## Adaptation / related text notes

Original. Pair with ARQ / Happy Death Day 2U for nested loops; with *Dark* for cult-time theology; with *Russian Doll* for death-reset psychology.
