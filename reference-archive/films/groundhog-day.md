# Groundhog Day (1993)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Loop-day calendars and ‘how many years’ charts are a staple; exact loop count is famously debated.

## Short blurb

Cynical weatherman Phil Connors relives the same February 2 in Punxsutawney until moral change (and love) breaks the loop.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

A private home in Woodstock, Illinois , was used for exteriors of the Cherry Street Inn, the fictional location in which Phil awakens every morning in the film. On February 1, cynical television weatherman Phil Connors reassures his Pittsburgh viewers that an approaching blizzard will miss Western Pennsylvania . Alongside his new producer Rita Hanson and cameraman Larry, Phil travels to Punxsutawney for his annual coverage of the Groundhog Day festivities.

He makes no secret of his contempt for the assignment, the small town, and the "hicks" who live there, asserting that he will soon leave his station for a new job. On February 2, Phil awakens in the Cherry Street Inn to Sonny & Cher 's " I Got You Babe " playing on the clock radio. He gives a half-hearted report on the groundhog Punxsutawney Phil and the festivities. Contrary to his prediction, the blizzard strikes the area, preventing all travel, and although he desperately searches for a way to leave, he is forced to spend the night in the town.

The next morning, Phil again awakens to "I Got You Babe" and the same DJ banter on the radio in his room at the Cherry Street Inn. He experiences the previous day's events repeating exactly and believes he is experiencing déjà vu . He again unsuccessfully attempts to leave the town and retires to bed, only to awaken on February 2 once more. Phil realizes that he is trapped in a time loop of which no one else is aware.

He confides his situation to Rita, who directs him to a neurologist, who in turn directs him to a psychologist; neither can explain his experiences. He gets drunk with locals Gus and Ralph and then leads police on a high-speed car chase before being arrested and imprisoned; the next morning, Phil awakens in the Cherry Street Inn once again. Realizing that there are no consequences for his actions, Phil begins to spend his loops indulging in binge eating, one-night stands, and robbery, using his growing knowledge of the day's events and the town's residents to manipulate circumstances to his advantage.

He eventually focuses on seducing the sweet-natured Rita, using the loops to learn more about her and exploit that knowledge. No matter what steps he takes, Rita rebuffs his advances, particularly when Phil tells her he loves her; she asserts that he does not even know her. Phil gradually becomes depressed and desperate for a way to escape the loop. He commits suicide in a variety of ways, including kidnapping Punxsutawney Phil and driving them both off a cliff.

Each time, he reawakens on February 2. He tries to explain his situation to Rita again, using his detailed knowledge of the day to predict events accurately. Convinced, Rita spends the day with him and encourages him to view the loop as a blessing rather than a curse. As they lie on the bed together at night, Phil realizes that his feelings for Rita have become sincere. He wakes alone on February 2. He decides to use the loop to change himself and help others: he saves people from deadly accidents and misfortunes and learns to play the piano, sculpt ice, and speak French.

Despite his efforts, however, he is haunted by his inability to prevent a homeless old man from dying of natural causes. During one iteration of the loop, Phil reports on the Groundhog Day festivities with such eloquence that other news crews stop working to listen, amazing Rita. Phil continues his day helping the people of Punxsutawney. That night, Rita witnesses Phil's expert piano-playing as the adoring townsfolk regale her with stories of his good deeds.

Impressed by his apparent overnight transformation, Rita successfully bids for him at a charity bachelor auction. Phil carves an ice sculpture in Rita's image and tells her that no matter what happens, even if he is trapped in the loop forever, he is finally happy because he loves her. They share a kiss and retire to his room. Phil wakes the next morning to find Rita still in bed with him; it is now February 3.

He tells Rita he wants to live in Punxsutawney with her.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

Loop structure (film + public commentary):

1. **Entry:** Phil covers Groundhog Day; blizzard traps crew; he wakes to the same radio cue (“I Got You Babe”) and identical town events.
2. **Exploitation phase:** Theft, seduction scripts, reckless driving — consequentialism without lasting consequence.
3. **Despair phase:** Suicide attempts; still wakes to the same morning — loop is not escapable by death.
4. **Learning / virtue phase:** Piano, French, ice sculpture, medical saves; genuine care for townsfolk; repeated failed then sincere courtship of Rita.
5. **Perfect day:** Phil’s altruism aligns with Rita’s values; overnight stay; morning is February 3 — loop exits.

No on-screen machine: metaphysical/moral closed timelike curve. Harold Ramis and Bill Murray interviews (public) discuss unspecified long duration; fan estimates range from weeks to thousands of years — archive notes the debate without picking a number as fact.

## Paradox analysis

- **Closed time loop (repeating day):** External world resets; Phil’s memory and skill persist — information asymmetry defines the genre template for later loops (*Palm Springs*, *Edge of Tomorrow*, *Russian Doll*).
- **Not a bootstrap identity loop:** Phil does not cause his own birth; he causes his own ethical rewrite.
- **Exit condition as moral/romantic:** Unlike sci-fi failsafe exits, the film ties escape to character change — debated as fantasy ethics rather than physics.
- **Suicide non-exit:** Establishes loop robustness; death is not a loophole.
- **Knowledge without branching:** Each day overwrites public history; only Phil (and eventually Rita for one night) carries continuity.
- **Template status:** TVTropes “Groundhog Day Loop” names the pattern used by dozens of later works.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

1993 theatrical classic; National Film Registry recognition in public records. Multiple home releases. No theatrical alternate ending that changes the loop model. Soundtrack cue “I Got You Babe” is the reset bell.
