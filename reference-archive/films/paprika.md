# Paprika (パプリカ, 2006)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Dream-layer / DC Mini theft maps and parade-invasion flowcharts are common fan explainers; often paired with *Inception* influence charts.

## Short blurb
Therapist Atsuko Chiba treats patients inside dreams as her alter ego Paprika—until stolen DC Mini devices let a “dream terrorist” merge nightmare parades with waking Tokyo.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
(Source: English Wikipedia plot.) Near-future Institute for Psychiatric Research develops the **DC Mini**, which lets users view and enter dreams. Dr. **Atsuko Chiba** illegally treats clients as dream-detective **Paprika**, aided by department chief **Toratarō Shima** and inventor **Kōsaku Tokita**. Client detective **Toshimi Konakawa** has a recurring cinematic nightmare tied to a repressed friend/homicide case.

Three DC Mini prototypes are stolen. Shima, hijacked mid-meeting, leaps through a window while ranting; his dream is a chaotic **parade of objects**. Suspect assistant **Kei Himuro** proves an empty shell. Chairman **Seijirō Inui**, hostile to the device, is the true culprit, aided by jealous staffer **Morio Osanai**, who wants to “protect” dreams from humankind. Scientists fall victim; Tokita is swallowed by the parade. Paprika is captured; Osanai peels her skin to reveal Atsuko, then is killed in Konakawa’s dream when the detective resolves his guilt and shoots Osanai’s dream-body—killing the real Osanai.

Dreams and reality merge: the parade runs amok in the city. Paprika appears as a fully separate entity from Atsuko. Giant-robot Tokita eats Atsuko; Inui returns as a megalomaniacal nightmare giant. Paprika enters Tokita’s body; a baby emerges, ages into Atsuko–Paprika fused, and consumes Inui, ending the nightmare. Atsuko sits by Tokita’s bedside as he wakes. Konakawa visits Paprika’s website card and goes to see *Dreaming Kids*.

## Timeline/structure notes
- Not machine time travel: **interacting dream-realities** that rewrite waking matter—archive-adjacent parallel ontology.
- Elevators, hallways, and film-genre dream beats map layers of Konakawa’s psyche.
- Duality Atsuko/Paprika is Kon’s stated theme of contrast within one person.
- Frequently cited as visual/thematic precursor to Nolan’s *Inception* (critics note shared dream-share tech, hallway physics, elevators); Nolan has not directly confirmed.

## Paradox analysis
- Dream-death can kill the waking body (Osanai).
- Collective nightmare can overwrite city-scale reality.
- Identity peel: Paprika and Atsuko become separable agents mid-climax.
