# Project Almanac (2015)

## Short blurb
MIT-bound teen David Raskin finds DARPA “Project Almanac” blueprints in his late father’s basement and builds a time machine with friends. Lottery wins and romance rewrites cascade into plane crashes and blackouts; David burns the past to unmake the machine—yet a second camera hints the loop of discovery continues.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

David, sister Christina, and friends Adam and Quinn dig through inventor father Ben’s effects (Ben died in a car crash on David’s seventh birthday) for scholarship material. A birthday-party videotape shows future teen David reflected in a mirror reaching for a basement light—leading them to sealed plans for a DARPA time machine. Using Jessie Pierce’s hybrid car fuel cell as power, they send a toy car back and black out the neighborhood; Jessie joins.

Rules emerge: solo jumps forbidden after Quinn draws on his sleeping past self and nearly erases both in a feedback loop when past-Quinn wakes. Group uses three-week range for personal gains—lottery (second prize after a wrong number), bully revenge, science presentation redo—then Lollapalooza three months back. David solo-jumps to fix his Jessie confession, creating a coupled future.

Ripples worsen: plane crash, news disasters, friendship fractures, Jessie breaks up after learning the truth. David realizes the machine must never exist. He jumps to his seventh birthday, destroys blueprints and materials in the basement (found-footage camera records him vanishing from the frame as the timeline rewrites). Present-day attic: Christina finds *two* cameras—the original party tape and the full chronicle of their travels. Future-knowing David reintroduces himself to Jessie, implying knowledge persists or a new cycle begins.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Discovery via future reflection** on past tape (bootstrap image).
2. **Build / toy-car proof / Jessie recruit.**
3. **Past-self feedback near-erase** → no-solo rule.
4. **Wish-fulfillment hops** (lottery, concert, romance solo break).
5. **Butterfly disasters** escalate global cost.
6. **Birthday burn** — David erases machine & himself from that branch’s future.
7. **Dual-camera coda** — paradoxical residual evidence + flirt restart.

Found-footage grammar makes the camera both document and temporal artifact.

## Paradox analysis

- **Branching youth TT:** Changes rewrite the present; disasters = butterfly bill.
- **Past-self contact:** Feedback erasure when two Quinns meet—hard identity conservation.
- **Future-on-past tape:** Seeing future David in seventh-birthday footage is the classic bootstrap lure that causes the machine’s rediscovery.
- **Self-erasure ending:** Burning plans removes future-David from the birthday room on camera—yet the travel chronicle camera survives into the attic (residual object problem debated on SciFi.SE).
- **Ambiguous epilogue:** Does Jessie meet a David who still remembers, or is it first contact with eerie confidence?

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Paramount / Platinum Dunes (Bay); found-footage teen thriller delayed from 2014. Runtime ~106 min. Often compared to *Chronicle* + *Butterfly Effect*. Ending intentionally leaves camera ontology fuzzy.
