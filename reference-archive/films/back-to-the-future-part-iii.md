# Back to the Future Part III (1990)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Fan/StackExchange reconstructions of the Clara/tombstone timeline versions (A/B/C) are the standard structural diagrams for this Western closer.

## Short blurb
Stranded in 1955 after Part II, Marty learns Doc was sent to 1885 and will be murdered by Buford “Mad Dog” Tannen. He rides the repaired DeLorean into the Old West, loses gasoline, and must push the car to 88 mph with a stolen locomotive—while Doc falls for schoolteacher Clara Clayton.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

On **November 12, 1955**, moments after Doc vanishes in the lightning-struck DeLorean, Marty reads Doc’s 1885 letter: Doc is alive in the Old West, has buried the DeLorean for Marty, and begs Marty *not* to come rescue him. Marty and 1955-Doc retrieve and repair the buried car. At Boot Hill, Marty finds a tombstone: Doc dies September 7, 1885, shot by Buford Tannen over $80—“erected in loving memory by his beloved Clara.”

Marty overrides the letter and travels to **September 2, 1885**. He arrives amid a cavalry pursuit, tears the fuel line on an arrow, and is knocked out by a bear. Great-great-grandparents Seamus and Maggie McFly take him in. Under the alias **Clint Eastwood**, Marty reaches Hill Valley; Buford nearly lynches him until Doc intervenes. Learning of the tombstone, Doc agrees to leave—but without gasoline the DeLorean cannot hit 88 mph. They plan to use a steam locomotive on an unfinished spur over Shonash Ravine.

While scouting the spur, Doc saves **Clara Clayton** from plunging into the ravine (in the original history she dies and the ravine is renamed Clayton Ravine). They fall in love. At the festival Buford tries to shoot Doc; Marty intervenes and is goaded into a duel. The photograph of the tombstone updates: Doc’s name fades, implying Marty may die instead. Doc warns Marty about his future street-race accident with Needles.

Doc tries to break with Clara; she rejects the “from the future” story. Buford captures Doc, forcing the duel. Marty uses a stove-door “bulletproof vest,” defeats Buford (who is arrested), and clears the manure-gag trilogy beat.

Clara, on a train to San Francisco, overhears of Doc’s despair, returns, finds the model time machine, and races after the stolen locomotive pushing the DeLorean. As the train hits speed, Clara slips; Marty passes the hoverboard so Doc can save her. The DeLorean hits 88 mph and vanishes as the loco plunges off the unfinished bridge.

**October 27, 1985:** Marty escapes the DeLorean just before a modern freight train destroys it. He refuses Needles’s race—averting the accident—and Jennifer’s 2015 fax about Marty being fired goes blank. Doc returns in a **steam time-train** with Clara and sons Jules and Verne, gives Marty a photo from 1885, and tells them the future “hasn’t been written yet.” The ravine is now called **Eastwood Ravine**.

## Paradox analysis

- **Branching / ripple photography:** Tombstone and ravine names update as 1885 events change—same visual language as Parts I–II.
- **Not a pure closed loop:** Marty *does* change Doc’s death; “set right what once went wrong.”
- **Bootstrap-ish romance:** Clara’s presence on the stone requires a timeline where Doc met her; Marty’s interference reroutes *how* they meet but preserves the love story.
- **Gasoline gap:** Forces the train climax; ends Marty’s access to time travel when the DeLorean is destroyed.
- **Needles accident:** Knowledge from 2015/Doc’s slip lets Marty refuse the race—personal timeline rewrite, fax blanking as confirmation.

## Adaptations / related

Trilogy closer; Western pastiche written partly because Fox wanted Old West. Clara role written for Steenburgen (echoing her *Time After Time* romance with a traveler). No direct remake; Telltale game is Gale’s nearest “fourth film.”
