# Slaughterhouse-Five (1972)

## Short blurb
Billy Pilgrim becomes “unstuck in time,” shuffling among WWII Dresden, postwar optometry suburbia, and a Tralfamadorian zoo where aliens perceive all moments simultaneously—“so it goes.”

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

George Roy Hill’s adaptation follows Vonnegut’s non-linear life of Billy: drafted, captured, sheltered in Schlachthof-Fünf during the Dresden firebombing; later married, wealthy, surviving a plane crash; abducted to Tralfamadore with film-star Montana Wildhack. Editing *is* the time travel: scenes leap without machine UI. Tralfamadorians teach that all moments exist at once; free will is a human illusion. Death’s refrain “so it goes” punctuates. The film treats war trauma and fatalism as the true engines of temporal dislocation—grouped in TT discourse beside *Arrival*’s non-linear cognition debates.

## Paradox analysis

- **Consciousness-as-TT:** No box; the mind (or alien pedagogy) sequences eternity.
- **Predestination comfort:** Tralfamadorian eternalism vs human grief.
- **Formal paradox:** Cinema montage as literal time machine for the audience.
- **Not branching:** Moments are fixed; Billy visits, does not rewrite Dresden.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Universal; Cannes Jury Prize (shared). Michael Sacks as Billy. Closer to novel’s structure than many literary adaptations. Teachable as literary SF on film.
