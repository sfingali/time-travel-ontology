# Predestination (2014)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Ultimate on-screen bootstrap; identity/timeline diagrams are ubiquitous across explainers.

## Short blurb

A Temporal Bureau agent hunts the Fizzle Bomber and recruits a bitter writer — only to learn they are the same self-creating person: mother, father, child, agent, and bomber.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

In 1975, a time-traveling agent, whose face is not seen, suffers severe burns while trying and failing to disarm a bomb in a public building's basement. An unseen person helps him activate his time-travel device, called a "field kit", allowing him to escape to his agency's headquarters in 1992. He receives reconstructive surgery for his burns, but is told he will now look and sound different. He is further warned about the risk of mental instability from his long career.

Despite this, the agent is determined to capture the "Fizzle Bomber", the failed bombing's elusive perpetrator. Once he recovers, the agent is sent on his final mission before retirement and goes undercover as a bartender in 1970 New York City, where he meets John, a bitter columnist who writes under the pen name "The Unmarried Mother". When pressed on how he writes confession stories so well, John begins telling his life story.

A baby was found on the steps of a Cleveland orphanage in 1945, where she was taken in and given the name Jane. Jane excelled both physically and academically in high school, and upon graduation was recruited by Agent Robertson to join the space program as a concubine, but was disqualified after a medical examination. In college, Jane fell in love with a mysterious man, but was abandoned shortly into the relationship.

Jane went back to Robertson to try to join the space program, but Robertson admitted that he was using the space program as a front to recruit Jane for an elite covert agency that recruits people with no past and no certain future. Jane joined the agency, but was forced to drop out when she discovered she was pregnant. Jane gave birth to a baby girl at a hospital, telling the baby "you are the best thing that's ever happened to me", but, during the delivery doctors discovered that Jane was intersex .

Complications during delivery had rendered her female organs unviable, and the physicians began the process of gender reassignment surgery . Jane spent almost a year undergoing further treatment to present as male, during which time her baby was kidnapped from the hospital by an older man. After being rejected from the space program again, Jane renamed himself John and relocated to New York City, eventually becoming a columnist and still harboring resentment toward the man who impregnated and abandoned him.

When John finishes his story, the agent reveals that he works for the Temporal Bureau, in which Robertson is either a high-ranking officer or the head. He offers John the chance to kill his mysterious lover, who the agent thinks may be the Fizzle Bomber, in exchange for John joining the bureau as an agent. Together, they go back to 1963, where John encounters and falls in love with Jane (his past self), realizing that he was his past self's lover all along.

John, determined to change the past, goes ahead with the relationship and vows not to abandon himself as Jane. Meanwhile, the agent illegally returns to 1975 and helps his wounded past self, which Robertson allows as long as the agent kidnaps Jane's baby and delivers it to the Cleveland orphanage in 1945, completing the temporal paradox that makes John both of his own parents. Returning to 1963, the agent compels John to leave Jane and join the agency in 1985, as their troubled past is what will make them so effective at saving lives.

They travel to headquarters, where John passes out and is hospitalized. Informed his field kit will decommission after one final jump, the agent retires to New York City in 1975, but the kit fails to decommission. With information left for him by Robertson, he finds the Fizzle Bomber, only to his horror to discover that it is his future self. The bomber says that all his bombings were designed to prevent much greater disasters, and that the field kit still working is evidence that he was predestined to become the bomber.

The bomber gives the agent a chance not to kill him and break the cycle, but the agent shoots him. The agent records a message to be delivered to John at headquarters when he wakes up in 1985, repeating the line "you are the best thing that's ever happened to me" that Jane told her baby. He then takes off his robe to reveal scars from gender reassignment surgery, confirming he is an older John—an orchestrated paradox created by Robertson.

## Identity continuum (public reconstructions)

Explainers (Astronomy Trek, ThisIsBarry, Slashfilm, Wikipedia) treat the following as one continuum:

- **Baby Jane (1945 orphanage)** — left by the agent.
- **Jane** — orphan → athletic/academic standout → failed “space program” medical → romance with mysterious man → pregnancy.
- **John (“The Unmarried Mother”)** — after delivery reveals intersex anatomy, female organs unviable, gender reassignment; baby kidnapped; bitterness as confession-column writer in NYC.
- **Barkeeper / Temporal Agent** — recruits John; is older John after burn reconstruction.
- **Fizzle Bomber** — still-older self whose bombings (claimed as disaster prevention) the agent is assigned to stop.

Robertson engineers a recruit with “no past and no future” — perfect for temporal work because the loop supplies both origin and mission.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

Approximate public timeline consensus:

| When | Who / what |
|---|---|
| 1945 | Baby Jane abandoned at Cleveland orphanage (by the agent) |
| Early–mid 1960s | Jane grows; space-program front recruitment/rejection; meets John |
| 1963–64 | John/Jane romance; pregnancy; birth; surgery; baby taken to 1945 |
| 1970 | Barkeeper-agent meets John in NYC; hears the life story |
| 1975 | Attempted bomb disarm; severe burns; unseen helper activates field kit; later Bomber confrontation |
| 1985 | John forced to leave Jane; Bureau training begins |
| 1992+ | HQ recovery/surgery; missions; retirement kit that will not decommission |

Structural beats:

1. **Burn cold open** — establishes stakes and the unseen helper (future/past self).
2. **Bar confession** — nested autobiography that is already the loop.
3. **Offer of revenge** — agent takes John to 1963 to kill the lover; John *is* the lover and proceeds.
4. **Illegal 1975 assist** — agent helps burned past self escape.
5. **Baby relocation** — closes parental bootstrap.
6. **Recruitment coercion** — John begins Bureau path.
7. **Bomber reveal** — kit still works; predestination speech; agent kills Bomber and steps onto that path.
8. **Scar / message coda** — agent is older John; recording echoes Jane’s line to the baby.

## Paradox analysis

- **Bootstrap / ontological paradox:** DNA and personal identity have no external origin; the person is a closed causal loop (Heinlein’s “—All You Zombies—”).
- **Predestination / Novikov self-consistency:** Attempts to change (not abandoning Jane; killing the Bomber) *are* the history. StackExchange threads emphasize there is no initialization outside the loop.
- **Information bootstrap:** Bureau recruitment and Robertson’s planning are sustained by the loop they create.
- **Moral paradox:** Killing the Bomber both rejects and ensures the Bomber’s existence path; trauma of abandonment and forced reassignment is instrumentalized by the agency.
- **Single continuum, not branching:** The film offers recognition rather than an escape timeline — contrast with Primer’s revision arms race or *Dark*’s origin-world exit.
- **Title as thesis:** “Predestination” names the paradox type the plot enacts.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Theatrical ending is the Bomber confrontation plus scar reveal — no major alternate narrative cut is commonly cited. Ambiguity remains only in how much free will the agent retains; structure is closed. Sarah Snook’s dual performance was widely praised (AACTA Best Actress). Ethan Hawke anchors the agent/Bomber poles.

## Adaptation / related text notes

Spierig brothers adaptation of Robert A. Heinlein’s 1959 story “—All You Zombies—”. The film expands a Fizzle Bomber thriller frame around the short story’s bar confession. Heinlein’s title is spoken in narration. Often paired pedagogically with “By His Bootstraps.”
