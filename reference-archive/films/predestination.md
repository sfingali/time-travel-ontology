# Predestination (2014)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Ultimate on-screen bootstrap; identity/timeline diagrams are ubiquitous.

## Short blurb
A Temporal Bureau agent hunts the Fizzle Bomber and recruits a bitter writer—only to learn they are the same self-creating person: mother, father, child, agent, and bomber.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Adapted closely from Robert A. Heinlein’s 1959 story “All You Zombies—”. In 1975 an agent is burned failing to stop a bomb; an unseen helper activates his field kit to HQ in 1992. Reconstructive surgery changes his face/voice. On a final mission he bartends in 1970 NYC and meets John (“The Unmarried Mother”), who narrates Jane’s life:

- **1945:** Baby left at Cleveland orphanage → Jane.
- Excelled athletically/academically; recruited then rejected by a space-program front after medical exam.
- Falls for a mysterious man who abandons her; becomes pregnant.
- Delivery reveals Jane is intersex; female organs unviable; gender reassignment begins; baby kidnapped by an older man.
- Jane becomes John, moves to NYC, writes confession stories, burns with resentment.

The barkeeper-agent offers John a chance to kill the lover (suspected bomber) if John joins the Bureau. They jump to **1963**; John meets Jane and realizes he *is* the lover—and proceeds, determined not to abandon her. Meanwhile the agent illegally returns to **1975** to help his burned past self (himself), then—per Robertson’s orders—kidnaps Jane’s baby and leaves it at the **1945** orphanage, closing the loop that makes John both parents of himself. He then forces John to leave Jane and begin Bureau training in **1985**.

Retired to 1975 with a supposedly decommissioning kit that fails to shut down, the agent tracks the Fizzle Bomber and finds **his own future self**. The Bomber claims bombings prevent worse disasters and that the still-working kit proves predestination. The agent kills him—stepping toward becoming him. A recorded message to young John repeats Jane’s line to the baby: “you are the best thing that’s ever happened to me.” Removing his robe, the agent shows reassignment scars: he is older John. Robertson has engineered a person with “no past and no future”—perfect for temporal work.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure (public reconstructions)

Approximate public timeline (Astronomy Trek / ThisIsBarry / Wikipedia consensus):

| When | Who / what |
|---|---|
| 1945 | Baby Jane abandoned at orphanage (by agent) |
| 1960s | Jane grows; fails “space” medical; meets John |
| 1963–64 | John/Jane romance; pregnancy; birth; baby taken |
| 1970 | Barkeeper meets John; hears story |
| 1975 | Burn/bomb failure; agent helps past self; Bomber confrontation |
| 1985 | John recruited/trained after leaving Jane |
| 1992+ | HQ recovery; missions; identity as Jane→John→Agent→Bomber |

All major roles—Jane, John, barkeeper/agent, baby, Bomber—are one continuum.

## Paradox analysis

- **Bootstrap / ontological paradox:** DNA and identity have no external origin; the person is a closed causal loop.
- **Predestination / Novikov self-consistency:** Attempts to change (not abandoning Jane; killing Bomber) *are* the history. StackExchange and explainers emphasize there is no “initialization” outside the loop.
- **Information bootstrap:** Bureau recruitment and Robertson’s planning are sustained by the loop they create.
- **Moral paradox:** Killing the Bomber both rejects and ensures the Bomber’s existence path; trauma of reassignment and abandonment is instrumentalized by the agency.

## Adaptation notes

Spierig brothers stated they trusted Heinlein’s logic rather than “fixing” it. Film expands the Fizzle Bomber thriller frame around the short story’s bar confession. Sarah Snook’s dual performance was widely praised (AACTA Best Actress). Title quotes the paradox type; Heinlein’s title is spoken in narration.

## Endings

Theatrical ending is the Bomber confrontation plus scar reveal—no major alternate cut commonly cited. Ambiguity remains only in how much free will the agent retains; structure is closed.
