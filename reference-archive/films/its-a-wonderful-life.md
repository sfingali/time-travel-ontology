# It's a Wonderful Life (1946)

## Short blurb
Suicidal George Bailey is shown by angel Clarence an alternate Pottersville timeline in which George never existed—classic counterfactual “erase one life” branching without a machine.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

George Bailey (Stewart) spends a lifetime sacrificing travel dreams to save Bedford Falls’ Building & Loan. On Christmas Eve, facing scandal and ruin, he wishes he had never been born. Angel second-class Clarence Oddbody grants a **tourfactual tour**: without George, brother Harry drowned, the town is neon-noir Pottersville under Mr. Potter, Mary is unmarried, and Uncle Billy institutionalized. George begs to live again; reality restores; townspeople bail him out; Clarence earns wings. Not bodily time travel—**alternate-present vision**—but foundational to “what if I weren’t here” branching cinema.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Life montage** establishing butterfly seeds George planted.
2. **Crisis wish** “never born.”
3. **Pottersville walkthrough** — negative branch.
4. **Wish reverse / community restore.**

## Paradox analysis

- **Counterfactual branch:** Single-node removal cascade (It’s a Wonderful *Butterfly*).
- **No portal:** Divine vision frame; ontology is moral pedagogy.
- **Template status:** Ancestor of *Mr. Destiny*, *The Family Man*, many holiday alts; parallel-world grammar without SF chrome.

## Endings / production notes

RKO; box-office soft then public-domain TV ubiquity → canonization. Oscar nominations; AFI lists perennial. Based on “The Greatest Gift.”
