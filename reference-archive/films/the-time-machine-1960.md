# The Time Machine (1960)

## Short blurb
Victorian inventor H. George Wells (Rod Taylor) demonstrates a brass-and-crystal time machine, rides through world wars and nuclear ruin to 802,701 AD, finds childlike Eloi preyed on by subterranean Morlocks, and returns briefly for books before departing again to rebuild the future.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

London, New Year’s Eve 1899: George hosts skeptical friends (including Filby). He unveils the machine, makes a short demo, then—after they leave—embarks. Stop-motion/time-lapse shows fashion-changing shop windows, WWI, WWII Blitz, then a 1966 atomic war that entombs him in lava until the mountain erodes.

In 802,701 he finds the Eloi: beautiful, passive surface-dwellers who speak English fragments and fear nothing until sirens call them underground. Weena nearly drowns; George saves her. Exploring, he finds empty libraries and learns Morlocks farm Eloi as food. He descends, fights, recovers the stolen machine, and escapes as the Morlock caves burn.

He returns to 1900, tells Filby the tale, then vanishes again—Filby notes he took three books to teach the Eloi. The film’s Oscar-winning effects and romantic Weena plot soften Wells’s sharper satire while establishing Hollywood’s default time-machine iconography.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Dinner demo** — machine rules as stage magic.
2. **War montage tourism** — past/near-future as spectacle, not intervention.
3. **802,701 arrival** — Eloi idyll crack.
4. **Morlock revelation** — class/evolution horror.
5. **Rescue & return** — brief Victorian coda.
6. **Second departure** — open civilizing mission.

## Paradox analysis

- **Tourism, not rewrite:** George rarely changes historical wars; the future is destination.
- **Closed personal exit:** He leaves again—no permanent 1900 stay.
- **Allegory over physics:** Eloi/Morlock = decadent upper / laboring under classes after war.
- **Foundational cinema grammar:** Lever + spinning disks = the vernacular TT prop for decades.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

MGM; Academy Award for Special Effects. Often paired with 2002 Simon Wells remake for contrast. Influences *Time After Time*’s Victorian hardware look.
