# Tenet (2020)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Temporal pincer / turnstile diagrams dominate post-release explainers.

## Short blurb
A Protagonist joins a secret war over “inversion”—entropy reversed so people and objects move backward through time—racing oligarch Andrei Sator for Algorithm pieces that could annihilate the past.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

After a Kiev opera siege, the CIA agent called the Protagonist is recruited by organization Tenet. Scientist Barbara demonstrates inverted bullets that “return” to the gun. Handler Neil partners with him. Art-world plot: Sator blackmails wife Kat via a forged Goya; Freeport Oslo fight reveals a **turnstile** (entropy inverter). Priya explains Sator receives Algorithm instructions from a future seeking to bury the past over climate catastrophe.

Set pieces: amalfi yacht; Tallinn highway temporal pincer (forward vs inverted Sator/Protagonist); Freeport again with inverted Protagonist fighting his past self; Kat shot with inverted bullet requiring medical inversion care. Climax on the **14th**: Tenet’s red team (forward) and blue team (inverted) assault Stalsk-12 while Kat poses as her past self in Vietnam to hold Sator until the Algorithm is secured. Inverted Neil (orange trinket) dies unlocking a gate—later revealed as a sacrifice he always makes. Sator dies; Algorithm pieces are separated. Neil reveals he was recruited by a future Protagonist; their friendship is a bootstrap. The Protagonist will found Tenet.

## Mechanism: inversion vs jump travel

- **Turnstile:** reverses entropy; inverted beings experience time backward relative to environment; need oxygen apparatus; collisions risk “annihilation.”
- **Temporal pincer:** one team lives an operation forward; another inverts from after to approach with future knowledge—simultaneously.
- **Algorithm:** nine-section device to invert Earth permanently if assembled and triggered.
- Travel to earlier calendar dates requires *living backward* the intervening duration (or chaining missions)—not instantaneous DeLorean jumps.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

Public explainers (Empire, Independent, Medium timeline essays, Wikipedia) emphasize **calendar coincidence**: Kiev opera, Stalsk-12 battle, and Vietnam yacht suicide day share the 14th, revealed out of audience order. Neil’s life contains multiple overlapping selves at Stalsk (Wikipedia notes several simultaneous Neils). Tallinn sequence is the teaching pincer; Stalsk is the war pincer.

## Paradox analysis

- **Closed loop / bootstrap:** Tenet’s founding, Neil’s recruitment, inverted bullets’ provenance, and Neil’s death are self-originating from the Protagonist’s future.
- **Knowledge pincer:** Information flows from “later” experiences to “earlier” tactical advantage without branching escapes—Nolan’s single-continuum preference (akin to Prisoner of Azkaban / Tenet interviews’ “what’s happened happened” rhetoric in public coverage).
- **Free will tension:** Kat’s choice to kill Sator and Protagonist’s choice to spare certain players sit inside a determined structure.
- **Not Primer-style revision:** Inversion doesn’t overwrite; it overlays reverse traces on one history.

## Endings / cuts

Theatrical and home releases share the Neil reveal and “we have a future in our past” friendship. Some markets had pandemic-era delayed release; no radically different narrative cut is standard in sources. Sound mixing criticism noted dialogue intelligibility issues—not timeline variants.

## Adaptation notes

Original Nolan screenplay; palindrome title TENET; visual effects combined forward/backward photography. Score by Ludwig Göransson.
