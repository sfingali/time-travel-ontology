# Synchronicity (2015)

## Short blurb
Physicist Jim Beale’s two-run wormhole machine receives a genetically engineered Dahlia from “a week ahead.” To keep venture capitalist Klaus from owning him, Jim leaps through—and discovers he is sliding into parallel weeks where doubles decay and lover Abby’s “betrayal” may be a rescue.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Jim, Chuck, and Matty open one mouth of a wormhole using radioactive MRD from Klaus Meisner’s KMC; a unique Dahlia arrives as proof for the second run a week later. Klaus leverages funding into ~49% then nearly total ownership. Jim falls for mysterious Abby. Heartbroken and ownership-stripped, Jim dives through on the second run—“You may own the Dahlia, but you don’t own me”—arriving at the first-test week.

He is not in his own past but a **near-parallel**: he becomes the shadowy figure seen on first-test footage. Two Jims cannot stably coexist; the displaced Jim physically decays near his prime. Suspecting Abby–Klaus conspiracy, he maneuvers to reclaim the invention. Matty theorizes parallel timelines from Abby’s notebook paradox. Allies plan to use remaining MRD to send him on again.

Climax readings (This Is Barry / Culture Crypt): Abby’s apparent betrayal was to secure MRD and save him; a displaced Jim dies in a hotel; prime Jim, guided by notebook clues, alters how he first meets Abby (bar instead of lab), opening a healthier branch. Dahlia remains the causal token tying runs together.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Run A:** Open receiver; Dahlia arrives from “future.”
2. **Deal with Klaus / meet Abby.**
3. **Run B:** Jim self-sends; lands in parallel week-of-Run-A.
4. **Double decay / conspiracy paranoia.**
5. **Notebook / Matty parallel theory.**
6. **Sacrifice & meet-cute rewrite** for prime continuity.

Neo-noir visuals deliberately evoke *Blade Runner*; plot kinship with *Primer* and *Timecrimes*.

## Paradox analysis

- **Two-run wormhole:** Object (Dahlia) must be sent on run B to justify arrival on run A—classic bootstrap flower.
- **Parallel, not pure past:** Explains why “changing” things doesn’t cleanly overwrite memories.
- **Exclusion principle:** Doubles induce decay/death—conservation of identity across nearby worlds.
- **Ownership as motive:** Capitalist IP fight drives the leap more than curiosity.
- **Romantic uncertainty:** Abby reads as femme fatale until late reframe—trust as timeline variable.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Low-budget US indie; limited theatrical / VOD 2015–16. Runtime ~101 min. Dense for its budget; explainers disagree on exact Jim-count. Strong cult following among Primer fans.

## Adaptation / related text notes

Original. Teach beside Primer for “inventor vs funder” TT and beside Coherence for parallel-identity cost.
