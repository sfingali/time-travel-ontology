# Primer (2004)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** One of the most diagrammed time-travel films ever — intersecting-timeline graphs are practically required reading.

## Short blurb
Two Dallas engineers accidentally invent a Box that sends a person back several hours. Stock-market experiments fracture into competing timelines, failsafe machines, and multiple overlapping versions of Abe and Aaron.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Abe and Aaron, with friends in a garage tech side-project, notice anomalous mass/gravity effects around a device intended for other R&D. They realize that a person who enters the insulated Box after it has been running can exit near the moment it was switched on—having waited inside for roughly the duration of the desired backward trip. Public explainers (Astronomy Trek, qntm, Film Colossus) stress four operating rules:

1. The Box only returns you to its own start time—not arbitrary past.
2. You must occupy the Box for about as long as you wish to travel back.
3. During the overlapped hours, earlier and returned selves coexist.
4. Protocol demands hiding from your earlier self to preserve “symmetry.”

They initially use short daily trips to profit from after-hours stock knowledge, living in hotels to avoid doubles. Abe, more cautious, secretly starts a **failsafe Box** days earlier so he can, if needed, return to before Aaron knew about time travel and shut the project down.

Things escape control. **Thomas Granger**—father of Rachel—appears in a state that implies he has used a Box, though neither Abe nor Aaron yet understands how he learned of it. Carruth has discussed this as possible “recursion”: a future telling causes a journey that undermines the telling. Granger’s collapse pushes Abe to use the failsafe.

Abe exits earlier in the week, drugs his earlier self, and tries to prevent Aaron from ever learning of the Box. He fails: **Aaron had already found Abe’s failsafe**, used it, and brought folded Boxes. Aaron builds his own earlier failsafe and replaces Abe’s, giving himself a head start—whenever both travel, Aaron can arrive first. Aaron records conversations and wears an earpiece to lip-sync prior passes, iteratively rewriting the confrontation at Robert’s party involving Rachel’s ex and a shotgun. Narration asks how many revisions it took—“Three? Four? Twenty?”—and invokes “the actuality, the last revision.”

By the end, multiple Aarons and Abes occupy overlapping calendar time. One Aaron leaves to build a much larger machine abroad; Abe stays to sabotage continuing experiments by earlier selves, knowing containment may be impossible. Friendship is destroyed by asymmetric secrecy.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

- **Invention & proof:** Weeble/watch tests; first human trips; binocular proof of doubles.
- **Stock protocol:** Shared, “symmetric” short loops.
- **Failsafe divergence:** Abe’s secret insurance vs Aaron’s discovery and preemption.
- **Granger anomaly:** Proof of unknown future use; trust collapse.
- **Party recursion:** Aaron’s iterative destiny manipulation of a single night.
- **Exit vectors:** Containment (Abe) vs scale-up (Aaron).

Diagram culture (Gizmodo/Unreality “definitive graph,” Astronomy Trek multi-diagram essay, qntm scene-by-scene, Film Colossus “giant explanation”) disagrees on exact Aaron counts but agrees on failsafe preemption and broken symmetry.

## Paradox analysis

- **Not a clean Novikov closed loop:** Returned agents revise events; Carruth has spoken of “multiple histories.”
- **Broken symmetry:** Contact/interference between selves creates divergent knowledge.
- **Bootstrap elements:** Recordings and foreknowledge feed later behavior; Granger may be recursive.
- **Ontology of duplicates:** Drugging an earlier self does not erase him—permanent co-presence is possible.
- **“Last revision”:** Suggests the world remembers the latest pass without cleanly deleting prior agents.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Shot on a famously tiny budget; Carruth performed writing, directing, starring, scoring, and editing. Deliberate technical jargon and overlapping dialogue create opacity that fans treat as a puzzle box. No theatrical alternate ending is widely cited; complexity is editorial and scripted, not cut-dependent. Upstream Color is Carruth’s follow-up (not TT).

## Adaptation / related text notes

Original screenplay. Frequently taught alongside Predestination, Timecrimes, and Dark as “homework” TT.
