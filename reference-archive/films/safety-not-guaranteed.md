# Safety Not Guaranteed (2012)

## Short blurb
Magazine intern Darius answers a classified ad seeking a time-travel partner—“Safety not guaranteed.” Grocer Kenneth may be a genius or delusional; government tails, a living “dead” ex, and a lake boat machine end with Darius and Kenneth vanishing—reality of travel left gloriously unproven.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Seattle magazine writer Jeff takes interns Darius (Plaza) and Arnau to Ocean View, WA, to profile the ad’s author—Jeff’s real motive is reconnecting with high-school flame Liz. The poster is **Kenneth Calloway**, a paranoid grocery clerk who believes government agents track him. Jeff alienates him; Darius infiltrates as a volunteer partner.

Training in the woods; Darius shares her mother’s death and says she wants to prevent it. Kenneth’s stated mission: return to 2001 to save girlfriend Belinda, killed when a car drove into her house. Jeff’s Liz reunion sours (she has a family; he faces adulthood). Darius and Kenneth fall for each other.

Whistleblower-ish government men and Jeff’s digging reveal Belinda is **alive**—Kenneth’s story cracks. He rationalizes that she lives *because* his prior jump succeeded (or that he caused the crash trying to save her, per some explainers). Jeff warns that agents are on the property; Kenneth flees to a boat-mounted machine on the lake. Darius confesses the journalism lie, says the rest was real, and boards. Kenneth says the mission is now only to go back for her. They activate and vanish while Jeff, Arnau, and agents watch. A filmed interview fragment plays over credits—still ambiguous.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Ad as inciting mystery** (text itself claims “I have only done this once before”).
2. **Journalism stakeout → Kenneth profile.**
3. **Training / trust / dual grief missions.**
4. **Belinda-alive reveal** — ontology crisis.
5. **Agents close in / boat launch.**
6. **Vanish ending** — travel or shared delusion escape?

Roger Ebert praised the film for sidestepping paradox homework: time travel is subject, not puzzle engine.

## Paradox analysis

- **Ambiguous realness:** Film never confirms the machine works—vanishing is the only “evidence.”
- **Self-serving rationalization:** Belinda alive can read as successful prior jump *or* Kenneth’s rewrite of a failed relationship (drunk-driver guilt theories in explainers).
- **Mission rewrite:** Love shifts target from Belinda to Darius—emotional closed loop without physics proof.
- **“Once before” claim:** Ad line is either bootstrap brag or lie; untested.
- **Government watchers:** Ground the paranoia without verifying physics.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Duplass brothers–adjacent indie; Sundance hit; Trevorrow’s feature debut before *Jurassic World*. Low budget; Plaza/Duplass chemistry carries. Frequently taught as “TT without diagrams.”

## Adaptation / related text notes

Inspired by a real early-internet classified parody ad. Not adapted from a novel. Tonal cousins: *Primer* (earnest inventors) without the homework; *Happy Accidents* (future-boyfriend maybe-liar).
