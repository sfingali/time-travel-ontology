# 12 Monkeys (1995)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Predestination diagrams tying airport death to childhood memory; La Jetée lineage maps.

## Short blurb
James Cole is sent from a post-plague future to find the virus’s origin; his “memories” of an airport shooting are the closed loop of his own death—inspired by Chris Marker’s La Jetée.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

A 1996–97 virus kills most of humanity; survivors live underground. Scientists send prisoners backward collecting information (not changing the past—official doctrine). Cole (Willis) is mis-landed in 1990, institutionalized with Jeffrey Goines (Pitt), diagnosed by Dr. Kathryn Railly (Stowe). Later correct landings lead Cole and Railly to chase the Army of the Twelve Monkeys—actually an animal-liberation red herring. True villain: Dr. Peters, assistant to a virologist, planning to release the virus at the airport.

Cole, increasingly unsure what is real, tries to flee into the past with Railly; at the airport he is shot by security—witnessed by a child who is young Cole. The scientists’ voice on a phone (“we found the virus”) confirms information salvage, not prevention. Cassandra tragedy: knowledge cannot avert the loop.

## Temporal structure
- Future → past missions with targeting error.
- Psychiatric present as foil for “is time travel real?”
- Airport as fixed point linking childhood memory to adult death.
- Army of 12 Monkeys as misdirection (Goines’s father’s lab animals).

## Paradox analysis
- **Predestination / closed loop:** The memory that motivates Cole is the death caused by the mission.
- **Information salvage model:** Future admits they cannot change past—only learn. Contrast Terminator’s branching attempts.
- **La Jetée adaptation:** Photo-roman of man sent to past whose childhood pier memory is his own death; Gilliam expands into feature thriller.

## Adaptation notes
Inspired by La Jetée (1962); not a shot-for-shot remake. Peoples screenplay. Pitt’s manic performance widely cited. Syfy later produced 12 Monkeys TV (2015–18) that *does* pursue change—see archive TV entry.
