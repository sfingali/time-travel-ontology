# Star Trek: First Contact (1996)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Borg/Federation chronology and Phoenix flight timelines are heavily diagrammed in Memory Alpha and fan essays.

## Short blurb
After a Borg cube assaults 24th-century Earth, a sphere escapes into a temporal vortex to stop Zefram Cochrane’s 2063 warp flight. USS Enterprise-E follows, fights assimilation of the ship, and ensures humanity’s first contact with Vulcans—the hinge of Federation history.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Picard dreams of Locutus trauma as Starfleet reports a new Borg attack. Ordered to the Neutral Zone (command fears his emotional compromise), Enterprise instead joins Earth’s defense. Picard targets a weak point on the cube; a sphere launches toward Earth and opens a temporal vortex. When the sphere vanishes, sensors show a Borg-assimilated Earth—proof the past was rewritten. Enterprise follows.

They arrive **4 April 2063**, day before Cochrane’s historic warp test that will draw Vulcan notice. The Borg seek to assimilate a post–World War III Earth and abort first contact. Enterprise destroys the sphere; an away team finds the Phoenix (built into a missile shell) damaged. Lily Sloane, Cochrane’s aide, is beamed up for radiation treatment. Picard and Data return to the ship suspecting Borg survivors; Riker stays to coach a reluctant, profit-minded Cochrane.

Borg infiltrate lower decks, assimilating crew and converting corridors. Data is captured. Lily holds Picard at gunpoint, then allies with him; they divert through a Dixon Hill holodeck program. Outside, Picard, Worf, and Hawk spacewalk to stop Borg from using the deflector as a beacon; Hawk is assimilated. As decks fall, Worf urges self-destruct; Picard’s Ahab-like obsession (“the line must be drawn here”) blinds him until Lily quotes *Moby-Dick* logic and shames him into evacuating the crew and arming auto-destruct while he stays for Data.

The Borg Queen grafts flesh onto Data, offering sensation for encryption codes. Picard offers himself in trade; Data appears to betray the ship, deactivates destruct, and fires on the Phoenix—then misses on purpose, ruptures coolant, and dissolves Borg organic components (and his new skin). Cochrane, Riker, and La Forge complete the warp flight. That night Vulcans land; Cochrane greets them. Timeline secured, Picard farewells Lily; Enterprise returns to the 24th century.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Present battle → sphere escape → assimilated-Earth glimpse** (proof of successful enemy rewrite).
2. **Arrival in 2063 Montana** — protect Phoenix / Cochrane.
3. **Split front:** planetside first-contact babysitting vs shipboard assimilation horror.
4. **Picard’s revenge arc** broken by Lily; self-destruct ordered.
5. **Queen / Data temptation** resolved by coolant purge.
6. **Phoenix warp + Vulcan landing** — origin moment preserved.
7. **Return** to uncorrupted future.

Early Renaissance Italy drafts were discarded as kitschy; writers fused Berman’s time-travel wish with Braga/Moore’s Borg desire. Borg Queen added after Paramount notes that a faceless Collective lacked drama.

## Paradox analysis

- **Enemy branching:** Borg success yields an Earth that never meets Vulcans—Federation erased.
- **Restoration mission:** Classic “set right what once went wrong” at civilizational scale.
- **Origin bootstrap (soft):** Crew must not replace Cochrane yet must ensure he flies—history’s hinge depends on their covert midwifery.
- **Personal vs temporal stakes:** Picard’s Locutus trauma is the character paradox; the timeline paradox is institutional.
- **Not closed-loop tourism:** They do not become legendary figures; they exit once Vulcans arrive.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Paramount; ~$45M budget; ~$146M worldwide; Frakes’s feature debut. Oscar-nominated makeup; multiple Saturn Awards (Spiner, Krige, costumes). Jerry Goldsmith score (Joel Goldsmith additional). First TNG film without TOS leads. Heavy ILM CGI + physical Enterprise-E miniature. Often ranked among the best Trek films (Ebert; later Forbes/Empire lists).
