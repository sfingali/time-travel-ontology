# Planet of the Apes (1968)

## Short blurb
Astronaut George Taylor’s relativistic cryosleep voyage ends on a world where apes rule mute humans—until the Forbidden Zone yields the Statue of Liberty, proving the planet is Earth’s far future after humanity’s fall.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Taylor, Landon, and Dodge leave Earth; Dodge dies, Landon is lobotomized after capture. Taylor (Heston) is caged, studied by chimpanzee scientists Cornelius and Zira, and hunted by orangutan orthodoxy under Dr. Zaius. He escapes with Nova toward the Forbidden Zone against ape taboo. Ruined human artifacts and finally the half-buried Statue of Liberty deliver the twist: not an alien world but post-apocalyptic Earth. Taylor’s curse—“You maniacs! You blew it up!”—seals the revelation.

Time travel here is **one-way future displacement** via relativity/cryosleep, not a machine you steer. Sequels (*Escape*) later send apes backward to the human past, expanding the franchise into bidirectional TT.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Launch & cryosleep** — Earth left behind.
2. **Crash / ape society** — misread as alien planet.
3. **Scientific sympathy vs orthodoxy.**
4. **Forbidden Zone trek.**
5. **Liberty twist** — calendar identity revealed.

## Paradox analysis

- **Future-as-alien:** Classic misrecognition; TT is the twist engine, not the weekly tool.
- **One-way ontology:** No return in the first film—despair ending.
- **Sequel branching:** Later entries add reverse travel and causality loops around ape origins.
- **Cold War allegory:** Nuclear self-destruction as temporal destination.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Fox; iconic Jerry Goldsmith score; groundbreaking ape makeup. Cultural landmark; multiple remake/reboot cycles. Often filed beside *The Time Machine* as foundational “future Earth” TT.
