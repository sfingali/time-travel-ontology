# Midnight in Paris (2011)

## Short blurb
Hollywood screenwriter Gil Pender, vacationing in Paris with materialistic fiancée Inez, is picked up at midnight by a 1920s car and parties with the Lost Generation. Falling for Picasso’s mistress Adriana, he learns every era nostalgizes an earlier one—and chooses present-day Paris (and antique dealer Gabrielle) instead.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

2010: Gil (Owen Wilson) and Inez (Rachel McAdams) vacation with her wealthy parents. Gil wants to move to Paris and finish a nostalgia-shop novel; Inez prefers rich-American tourism and is impressed by pedantic friend Paul.

Drunk after wine tasting, Gil walks alone; at midnight a vintage Peugeot collects him to a Jean Cocteau party—Zelda and Scott Fitzgerald, Hemingway, Cole Porter. Hemingway offers to show Gil’s novel to Gertrude Stein. Fetching the manuscript returns Gil to 2010 (laundromat where the café was).

He repeats the midnight pickup (Inez leaves before midnight). Stein critiques Picasso’s portrait of lover **Adriana** (Marion Cotillard); Gil is drawn to her shared longing for the past. Surrealists Dalí, Man Ray, and Buñuel accept his “from the future” claim without blinking; he pitches *The Exterminating Angel* to Buñuel. Inez’s father hires a detective.

Gil finds Adriana’s diary in a present bookstall predicting earrings and love; he buys earrings, gives them in the past. A horse-drawn carriage takes them to the **Belle Époque**; Toulouse-Lautrec, Gauguin, and Degas insist the *true* golden age was the Renaissance. Adriana is offered a ballet-costume job and wants to stay; Gil realizes endless nostalgia is a trap—“the present is always a little unsatisfying”—and returns alone. Detective takes a wrong turn into Louis XVI’s Versailles and flees revolutionaries.

Stein praises Gil’s rewrite; Hemingway says the protagonist doesn’t see his fiancée’s affair. Gil confronts Inez; she admits a fling with Paul. He breaks off the engagement and stays in Paris. At midnight by the Seine no car comes—instead he meets Gabrielle; they walk home in the rain, sharing love of Paris in the rain.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Present dissatisfaction / golden-age longing.**
2. **First midnight slip → 1920s salon network.**
3. **Repeat visits / Adriana romance / Stein workshop.**
4. **Belle Époque nested hop** — theme crystallizes.
5. **Break with Adriana & Inez.**
6. **Present chosen** — Gabrielle; rain; no further portal needed.

Allen wrote title-first; Gil rewritten as Californian for Wilson. Evokes Hemingway’s *A Moveable Feast*. Compared to *The Purple Rose of Cairo*’s unexplained magic.

## Paradox analysis

- **Whimsical portal, zero mechanics:** No device, no rules lecture—fantasy comedy.
- **Nested nostalgia:** Each epoch downgrades the prior “golden age”—thematic anti-paradox.
- **No causality crisis:** Gil’s suggestions (Buñuel) are cute bootstraps without world stakes.
- **Detective gag:** Time can snare the uninvited into lethal history.
- **Exit = maturity:** Stopping the midnight walk is character growth, not machine destruction.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Sony Pictures Classics; filmed entirely in Paris (Giverny, Versailles, Montmartre, etc.). Oscar Best Original Screenplay; nominations for Picture, Director, Art Direction. Allen’s highest-grossing film (~$151M on $17M). Faulkner estate sued over paraphrased “past is not even past” line—dismissed on fair use (2013).
