# Timecop (1994)

## Short blurb
TEC agent Max Walker polices illegal time travel while Senator Aaron McComb loots the past to buy the presidency—and once murdered Walker’s pregnant wife. Walker rewrites that night by forcing two McCombs to touch, erasing the senator and restoring Melissa.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Cold open, 1863: a futuristic raider massacres Confederates for gold—illegal time crime in embryo.

1994: Dr. Hans Kleindast’s invention lets travelers visit the past (not the future); changes ripple the present. The Justice Department forms the Time Enforcement Commission. Senator Aaron McComb oversees TEC funding; Commander Eugene Matuzak recruits detective Max Walker. Before Walker can join, home invaders attack; Melissa Walker dies in an explosion.

2004: Veteran agent Walker collars ex-partner Lyle Atwood in 1929 for Crash-era insider trading. Atwood implicates McComb’s campaign finance via temporal theft but will not testify. Walker, now suspicious, is paired with rookie Sarah Fielding. In 1994 they watch future-McComb warn young McComb not to sell his computer-chip stake, then murder partner Jack Parker. Future McComb states the film’s hard rule: **two instances of the same matter cannot coexist—contact means mutual destruction.** Fielding betrays Walker for McComb and is shot; McComb escapes.

Returning to 2004, Walker finds a rewritten present: McComb is a wealthy presidential frontrunner who has shut down TEC. Dying Matuzak helps Walker deduce McComb uses Kleindast’s original prototype. Walker jumps to 1994; Fielding is murdered before she can testify. Hospital records reveal Melissa was pregnant. Walker realizes he has arrived on the night of her murder, persuades her to keep younger Max home, and battles McComb’s henchmen with both Walkers present. Future McComb takes Melissa hostage; Walker lures **1994 McComb** into the house and forces the doubles together. They fuse and disintegrate, erasing McComb from history. Walker leaves Melissa with his younger self as the house explodes (survivable rewrite).

2004 restored: Matuzak and Fielding live; McComb vanished in 1994; Walker comes home to Melissa and their son.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **TEC premise:** Past-only travel; present as ripple surface.
2. **Inciting murder:** Melissa’s death locks Walker’s motive.
3. **1929 collar:** Atwood → McComb funding trail.
4. **1994 chip heist:** Future McComb tutors past self; same-matter rule stated.
5. **Present wipe:** TEC deleted; McComb ascendant.
6. **Prototype return:** Walker to murder-night 1994.
7. **Double confrontation:** Two Walkers vs two McCombs; forced merger erase.
8. **Restored domestic future.**

## Paradox analysis

- **Mutable past / living present:** Headlines and institutions rewrite when Walker returns—BTTF-style ripples.
- **Same-matter annihilation:** Explicit on-screen rule enabling the McComb erase (and warning against double contact).
- **Bootstrap crime:** Future knowledge of chip value creates McComb’s wealth that funds more travel.
- **Personal grandfathering:** Erasing McComb restores Melissa without Walker needing to “become” his past self permanently.
- **Not Novikov:** Multiple successful changes; final state is a constructed better branch.

Critics (Ebert et al.) flagged holes; the film still codifies 1990s action-TT grammar for comics adaptations.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Universal / Largo / Renaissance (Sam Raimi among producers); ~$27–28M budget; ~$100M+ worldwide—Van Damme’s highest-grossing lead. Mixed reviews (RT ~43%) but often cited among his best. Franchise: 1997 ABC series; 2003 DTV *Timecop 2: The Berlin Decision*; novelizations; SNES game; announced remakes unmade. Dark Horse origin comic differs in details (South African mine heist).
