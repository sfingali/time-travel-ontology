# Somewhere in Time (1980)

## Short blurb
Playwright Richard Collier wills himself from 1972 to 1912 via self-hypnosis at the Grand Hotel to find the elderly actress who once urged him to “come back to me.” He and Elise McKenna fall in love—until a modern coin breaks the trance; the pocket watch they share is a bootstrap.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

1972: College-grad Richard (Reeve) is approached by an elderly woman who gives him a watch and pleads “Come back to me.” Eight years later, burned-out playwright Richard vacations at Mackinac Island’s Grand Hotel, becomes obsessed with 1912 actress Elise McKenna (Seymour), and—advised by a professor—uses costume, removed modernities, and hypnotic will to arrive in 1912.

He courts Elise despite manager Robinson’s control. They fall in love; he gives her the watch (which she will one day give him—bootstrap). A 1979 penny falls from his pocket; modern date shatters belief; he is ripped back to 1972, wastes away of despair, and dies—reuniting with Elise in a white-afterlife tableau.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Elderly Elise plants the plea + watch.**
2. **Hotel obsession / hypnosis method.**
3. **1912 courtship vs Robinson.**
4. **Coin break / forced return.**
5. **Death reunion.**

Matheson adapted his novel *Bid Time Return*.

## Paradox analysis

- **Bootstrap watch:** Classic object with no origin.
- **Belief as engine:** Anachronism destroys the method—psychological TT.
- **Closed romantic loop:** Elder Elise remembers the affair Richard has yet to live.
- **No branching:** Single loop tragedy.

## Endings / production notes

Universal; modest box office, huge cable/afterlife cult; John Barry score beloved. Reeve/Seymour chemistry iconic. Insomniac-hotel pilgrimage tourism on Mackinac.
