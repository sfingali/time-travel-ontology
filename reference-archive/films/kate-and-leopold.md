# Kate & Leopold (2001)

## Short blurb
Amateur physicist Stuart pulls his duke ancestor Leopold from 1876 New York through a Brooklyn Bridge portal. Leopold romances Stuart’s ex Kate while elevators “occlude”; she must leap back through the Monday window—becoming Leopold’s bride and, in uncut versions, Stuart’s great-great-grandmother.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

**28 April 1876:** Orphaned Duke Leopold of Albany sketches the unfinished Brooklyn Bridge and notices Stuart Besser photographing with an anachronistic camera. At the ball where Leopold must announce a wealthy bride, he chases Stuart onto the bridge; both fall through a gravitational time portal into **2001** Manhattan (88 White Street).

Stuart explains the portal reopens Monday; Leopold must stay put. Walking the dog, Stuart falls down an elevator shaft, rants about his discovery, and is committed. Per Stuart’s theory, Leopold’s presence causes citywide elevator failures and may erase Stuart unless Leopold returns.

Leopold meets cynical market researcher Kate McKay (Stuart’s ex, downstairs). He charms her with eloquence about food, aces a margarine commercial audition, and literally rides to her rescue on a Central Park horse when her briefcase is stolen. Rooftop dinner, waltz, first kiss. He retrieves boyhood treasures (including his mother’s ring) from his still-standing childhood home at 1 Hanover Square. Integrity clash over the fake butter commercial; subdued Sunday.

Monday: Stuart escapes the asylum and sends Leopold home—elevators work again. Brother Charlie spots Kate in Stuart’s 1876 ball photo. At her promotion party (ironically at Hanover Square), Kate sees herself in Stuart’s pictures, abandons the speech, and with Stuart and Charlie races to jump from the bridge within minutes. She arrives in 1876; Leopold announces her instead of the heiress. Bridal waltz—Kate becomes Stuart’s great-great-grandmother in the director’s genealogy.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **1876 bridge chase / portal fall.**
2. **2001 orientation + Stuart’s injury/commitment.**
3. **Elevator occlusion as global symptom.**
4. **Romance week (Thu–Sun) with Monday deadline.**
5. **Leopold returned → photo proof Kate belongs in 1876.**
6. **Kate’s leap / ball announcement closed romance loop.**

## Paradox analysis

- **Portal window:** Fixed weekly Monday reopen—hard calendar constraint.
- **Presence side-effects:** Elevators fail while Leopold is displaced—whimsical conservation gag.
- **Bootstrap ancestor:** Director’s cut makes Kate Stuart’s lineal grandmother after their prior relationship—Ebert/Roeper objected; Miramax cut genealogy lines days before release.
- **Not mutable history tourism:** Past is visited; present romance resolves by Kate permanently emigrating to 1876.
- **Invention tease:** Leopold’s elevator sketches nod to Otis—soft technology bootstrap flavor without hard claim.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Miramax; ~$48M → ~$76M worldwide. Theatrical ~118 min vs director’s cut 123 min (DVD/Blu-ray restores incest-implication dialogue and Mangold cameo). Jackman Golden Globe–nominated; Sting’s “Until…” won Golden Globe Best Song and was Oscar-nominated.
