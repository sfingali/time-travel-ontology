# Boss Level (2020)

## Short blurb
Retired Delta operator Roy Pulver is trapped in a ~140-day death loop engineered by his scientist wife’s Osiris Spindle. Assassins, a noon hard-fail, and Colonel Ventor’s bid for dictatorship force Roy to “finish the game”—save son Joe, stop Ventor, and reset the Spindle.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Roy wakes at 07:00, dodges apartment assassins and a helicopter gunner, flees before the building explodes, and dies every day at **12:47**—a pattern learned over ~140 loops. Flashback: estranged wife Jemma (Naomi Watts) lured him to Dynow Labs under a job pretext; Col. Clive Ventor (Mel Gibson) threw him out; Jemma covertly sampled Roy’s hair.

Roy learns Jemma “died in a lab accident.” He finds son Joe at a gaming tournament in Underground Atlanta, bonds without revealing parentage, and dies protecting him from assassins—telling Joe he is his father as he dies. Realizing dental hygienist Alice planted a tracker, he removes it across trial loops and assaults Dynow. Ventor admits Jemma’s project is the **Osiris Spindle**—a quantum history rewriter Ventor wants for dictatorship—and that Ventor killed her; without Jemma the Spindle will eventually destroy the world.

Roy trains sword work (Michelle Yeoh’s Dai Feng, loop by loop), kills Ventor in one pass, but assassins murder Joe; the Spindle detonates apocalypse. Despondent loops follow; then Roy discovers Jemma phoned Joe *after* Ventor’s supposed murder time—she was still alive early that morning. He hijacks the chopper, reaches Dynow in fourteen minutes, stops Ventor, and reunites with Jemma. She says only Roy entering the Spindle can reset it: he may get one more day or die permanently. He kisses her and prepares to enter.

**Two ending cuts circulated:** (1) Roy enters as Jemma watches; (2) Roy faces the morning attacker again with “Piece o’ cake,” fate unresolved. Viewer chooses.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Tutorial deaths:** Map the day’s assassin choreography (explicit video-game “level” metaphor; title = boss fight).
2. **Emotional sidequest:** Day with Joe; father reveal at death.
3. **Tracker removal / Dynow infiltrate:** Learn Spindle lore from Ventor mid-fight.
4. **False victory:** Ventor dead, Joe dead, world ends—fail state that teaches the real win condition.
5. **Despair loops → research loops:** Jemma’s late phone call reframes the morning as a rescue speedrun.
6. **Speedrun ending:** Chopper route; save Jemma before the fourteen-minute mark.
7. **Spindle reset / ambiguous continue:** Enter machine or one last “life.”

Carnahan developed the project as *Continue* (2012) for Fox—literally “Groundhog Day as an action movie” with game-continue grammar.

## Paradox analysis

- **Death-triggered reset:** Standard action-looper (*Edge of Tomorrow*, *Happy Death Day*) with explicit game grammar (bosses, continues, speedruns).
- **Roy as battery:** Jemma placed him in the Spindle—operator is also the loop fuel (compare ARQ’s device ontology).
- **Mutable within the day only:** No multi-day travel; stakes are optimizing one calendar day before global detonation.
- **Joe as fail condition:** Emotional binding turns “win the level” into “protect the child.”
- **Ambiguous exit:** Dual endings refuse a single ontology for post-Spindle reality.
- **Knowledge persistence:** Roy keeps skills (sword, routes) across deaths—skill XP without inventory carry.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

Developed for Fox as *Continue* (2012); screen tests with Grillo posted when Fox stalled. Revived 2017 with Gibson. Shot Georgia (Savannah/Atlanta) 2018; ~$45M budget. Theatrical plans with Entertainment Studios collapsed; Hulu US release March 2021 after ~$11.75M acquisition. Blumhouse later sued over an unpaid recut (settled). Runtime 100 minutes.
