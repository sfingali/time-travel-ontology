# Berkeley Square (1933)

## Short blurb
American Peter Standish walks into his ancestral London house and slips from 1933 into 1784, living his ancestor’s life and loving Helen—only to return stranded by history, aging into the man who will send his diary forward.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Leslie Howard stars as modern Peter who inherits a Berkeley Square house and becomes obsessed with an 18th-century namesake ancestor’s diary. Crossing a threshold at a matched hour, he arrives in 1784 in the ancestor’s body/role, startling the household with anachronistic knowledge. He falls for Helen Pettigrew but cannot escape the scripted marriage politics of the age; his “modern” sensibility marks him as uncanny. Forced back to 1933, he is left mourning Helen—while implication holds that the diary and house continue the circuit. Pre-Code Hollywood prestige fantasy; Howard Oscar-nominated.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Modern obsession with diary/house.**
2. **Threshold slip into 1784 society.**
3. **Romance vs period constraint.**
4. **Return and lifelong grief.**

## Paradox analysis

- **Body/role occupancy:** Early “walk into ancestor” template later echoed by *Somewhere in Time*’s belief method and body-swap adjacent tales.
- **Diary bootstrap flavor:** Document that inspires the trip may be written because of the trip.
- **Melancholy closed feeling:** Romance cannot permanently relocate either lover.

## Endings / production notes

Fox Film Corporation. Remade loosely as *I’ll Never Forget You* (1951). Landmark early talkie time-travel romance.
