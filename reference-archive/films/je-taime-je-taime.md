# Je t'aime, je t'aime (1968)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Non-linear shard structure; cited influence on *Eternal Sunshine* and memory-as-edit cinema.

## Short blurb
After a suicide attempt, Claude Ridder agrees to a Crespel lab’s one-minute past revisit; the mouse-proven capsule instead splatters him through disordered flashes of his life with Catrine—including her ambiguous Glasgow death.

## Detailed plot summary
(Source: English Wikipedia; deepen pass.) Researchers confident from animal tests recruit Ridder. Instead of one controlled minute from a year earlier, editing becomes the time machine: fragmented seconds/minutes of mundane and catastrophic beats with melancholic Catrine. Ridder may or may not have allowed her gas-heater death. After roughly an hour of subjective shards, staff believe him lost; they find him outside shot through the heart as if the suicide attempt replayed as exit. Ambiguous survival; tear on cheek. Manohla Dargis and others read trips as memory itself. Listed for cancelled 1968 Cannes. Sternberg’s SF script + Resnais’s *Hiroshima mon amour* / *Last Year at Marienbad* grammar.

## Timeline/structure notes
- Editing = temporal logic; no clean HUD clock.
- Failed protocol → involuntary life review.
- Soft predestination of the suicide wound.
