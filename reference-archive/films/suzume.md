# Suzume (すずめの戸締まり, 2022)

## Short blurb
Seventeen-year-old Suzume Iwato helps closer Souta Munakata seal abandoned doors across Japan that release a catastrophic "worm"; their road trip ties personal grief from the 2011 Tōhoku disaster to a supernatural Ever-After.

## Detailed plot summary
(Source: English Wikipedia.) Suzume meets Souta searching abandoned sites for doors. At a ruined onsen she opens a freestanding door onto a starlit field she cannot enter and knocks over a cat-shaped keystone (Daijin), which becomes a living cat and flees. A smoke-column "worm" emerges; Suzume helps Souta lock the door with an old key, stopping local quake-like damage. Daijin turns Souta into the three-legged chair he sat on. They chase Daijin via ferry to Ehime, then Kobe and Tokyo, sealing doors at an abandoned school and amusement park. Souta explains Closers imprison the worm using western and eastern keystones; Daijin was the western keystone. In Tokyo Daijin transfers the keystone role to Souta; Suzume must use chair-Souta to seal a Tokyo emergence. To rescue him she learns she once entered the Ever-After as a child through a unique door in her Tōhoku hometown destroyed in 2011. With aunt Tamaki (temporarily hosting eastern keystone Sadaijin) and Souta's friend Serizawa, she reaches the ruins, re-enters the Ever-After, reunites with Souta, and—after Daijin accepts keystone duty again—re-imprisons the worm. Young Suzume in the Ever-After mistakes older Suzume for her late mother; older Suzume gives the childhood chair so younger Suzume can leave and be raised by Tamaki. A year later Suzume meets Souta again on the road where they first met.

## Timeline/structure notes
- Third of Shinkai's informal "disaster trilogy" (Your Name, Weathering with You, Suzume).
- Ever-After as afterlife/space accessed via abandoned-place doors; childhood self encounter closes an emotional loop.
- Not classical machine time travel; interacting otherworld + memory-time.
