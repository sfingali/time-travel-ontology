# Millennium (1989)

## Short blurb
A thousand years hence, sterile humanity snatches passengers from doomed airliners and replaces them with pre-burned replicas. NTSB investigator Bill Smith and physicist Arnold Mayer dig into crash anomalies until operative Louise Baltimore’s mission—and Mayer’s death—trigger a “force infinity” timequake that forces evacuation through the Gate.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

1989: Midair disaster. A flight engineer’s CVR claim that passengers are already burned corpses baffles NTSB lead Bill Smith. Theorist Dr. Arnold Mayer lectures on possible time visitors and privately holds a stun weapon recovered from a 1963 crash.

~1000 years later: Pollution has destroyed human fertility. Teams abduct people about to die in the past, hold them in stasis, and plan to send them to a farther future to repopulate. Operatives (often women, healthier than the atrophied council) smoke to tolerate cleaner present air. Each incursion causes a **timequake** sized to the disruption; snatch teams minimize ripples by leaving organic replicas—pre-charred for crash sites—explaining the engineer’s report.

In 1963 a traveler is shot, losing a stunner that reaches Mayer and aims his life’s work. In 1989 Smith finds a similar artifact. Louise Baltimore is sent to distract Smith—seduction and misdirection—lest his investigation change history. Smith grows suspicious, visits Mayer; Louise materializes and confesses the mission. While reassembling the weapon, Mayer dies.

Mayer was crucial to inventing Gate technology; his death creates an unsolvable paradox—a **force infinity timequake**—that will annihilate the future timeline. The only option: push all warehoused abductees through the Gate into the far future before permanent destruction. Bill and pregnant Louise step through together. Android advisor Sherman quotes Churchill as the Gate explodes: not the end, but the end of the beginning.

Alternate international ending adds Edenic underwater imagery after the wormhole; North American theatrical/VHS used a simpler sunrise.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Crash anomaly / CVR puzzle** (replicas already dead).
2. **Future ethics:** salvage the doomed; replicas; cigarettes as atmosphere sim.
3. **Artifact trail:** 1963 stunner → Mayer; 1989 find → Smith.
4. **Baltimore distraction mission** fails into confession.
5. **Mayer’s death** = Gate inventor paradox.
6. **Force infinity evacuation** through the dying Gate.

Production spanned a decade of rewrites (Varley cited four directors before Anderson); Trumbull/Newman/Fonda attachments fell through after *Brainstorm* turmoil.

## Paradox analysis

- **Snatch ethics vs Novikov:** They change who dies in crashes but try to leave physics of the crash intact via replicas—soft concealment of intervention.
- **Timequakes:** Explicit on-screen cost function for timeline stress.
- **Bootstrap tech:** Mayer’s present research enables the future Gate that depends on him—his premature death breaks the chain.
- **Force infinity:** Narrative maximum paradox—uncorrectable inventor loss.
- **Spiritual cousin to *12 Monkeys*:** salvage/quarantine futurism without Gilliam’s dream logic.
