## Blurb
Hugo-winning novella: agent Pierce serves Stasis—an organization that reseeds Earth with humans after every extinction by jumping populations through deep time—until ties to the Opposition reveal that history itself is a palimpsest of overwritten worlds. Stross has called it an effective rewrite of Asimov's *The End of Eternity*.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
**Pierce** is a Stasis police officer. **Stasis** agents can travel through time; after each extinction event that ends humanity, they reseed Earth with a replacement population jumped from an earlier era, preserving a continuous (if overwritten) human presence across deep time. Over geological ages this creates a **palimpsest**—layers of erased civilizations beneath the official present.

An opposing organization, the **Opposition**, struggles against Stasis control. Pierce becomes entangled with Opposition purposes through personal and operational ties that the sparse public plot summary (Wikipedia stub) frames as gradual revelation rather than a single twist. The novella explores institutional immortality, the ethics of mass historical overwrite, and whether "preserving humanity" by continual reseeding is salvation or captivity.

Stross stated he rewrote *End of Eternity*'s core (bureaucratic time police; recruit; apparatus that must end for a freer future) without rereading Asimov first. Won **2010 Hugo Award for Best Novella**; collected in *Wireless*; Subterranean Press standalone 2011.
