## Blurb
Billy Pilgrim comes unstuck in time—jumping between WWII Dresden captivity, suburban optometry, and a Tralfamadorian zoo—while Vonnegut's semi-autobiographical anti-war mosaic insists "so it goes."

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
Metafictional Ch.1: narrator (Vonnegut) frames the Dresden book, Children's Crusade promise to Mary O'Hare, research trip with Bernard V. O'Hare.

**Billy Pilgrim**, Ilium NY: chaplain's assistant, captured in Battle of the Bulge with bully **Roland Weary** (who dies of gangrene blaming Billy; **Paul Lazzaro** vows revenge). Prisoners ride to Dresden, housed in **Schlachthof-fünf**; survive Feb 1945 firebombing in the deep cellar; forced to dig and burn corpses; friend **Edgar Derby** executed for stealing a teapot.

Billy is "unstuck": nonlinear jumps across birth, war, marriage to **Valencia**, optometry wealth, plane crash, Valencia's CO death en route to hospital, abduction to **Tralfamadore**—4D aliens who see all time at once, display Billy in a zoo geodesic dome, mate him with porn star **Montana Wildhack**. Tralfamadorian fatalism: all moments always exist; response to death is "So it goes." Free will is an Earthling illusion.

Barbara infantilizes postwar Billy; he speaks on radio about Tralfamadore; killed in 1976 by Lazzaro's assassin after predicting his death. Nested Kilgore Trout novels mirror abduction themes. War sections mostly linear; civilian life discontinuous. Classic PTSD-before-the-name reading; repeatedly banned/challenged.
