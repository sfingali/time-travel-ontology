## Blurb
Oxford historian Kivrin Engle is sent to what should be 1320—and lands in 1348 at the arrival of the Black Death—while a modern influenza epidemic cripples the time-travel project trying to retrieve her.

## Detailed plot summary
In mid-21st-century Oxford, historians observe the past via a net that "slips" or refuses paradoxes. Kivrin insists on a medieval drop; technician Badri collapses with a new flu after setting coordinates wrong. Kivrin arrives sick in 1348, is nursed at a manor, bonds with children Agnes and Rosemund and priest Father Roche, then watches plague annihilate the village. In 2054 Dunworthy fights bureaucracy and quarantine; Mary Ahrens dies treating patients; Gilchrist closes the net. Badri's error (not plague-from-past) is proven; Dunworthy and Colin finally retrieve surviving Kivrin. Hugo and Nebula winner; first of the Oxford time-travel sequence.

## Temporal structure
Institutional time travel with conservation laws (slippage, forbidden eras). Dual epidemic structure (past plague / present flu). Parallel narrative strands, not parallel universes.
