## Blurb
Posthumous Wells pastiche by Austrian polymath Egon Friedell: framed letters "to" H. G. Wells continue the Traveller's story—backward gear sticks, future run-ups, lunar Solonite interference, and satirical lectures on time.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
Written mid-1930s; published 1946 after Friedell's 1938 death (often titled *Die Reise mit der Zeitmaschine*; later editions *Die Rückkehr der Zeitmaschine*). Frame: Friedell writes Wells asking what became of the Traveller; a chilly secretary reply points to a witness, who supplies the continuation.

The Traveller tries for a modest past destination (~1840 lectures) but hits turbulence; he must first leap **forward** to gain "momentum" / intellectual toolkit (accounts vary ~2123–2138). Future Britain is wilderness; Egyptian visitors treat him oddly, offering hand-wavy physics (crossing lunar beings' world-lines / Solonites). Ambition escalates toward the beginning of time. Short, ironic, philosophically chatty sequel rather than adventure epic—early German-language engagement with Wells's machine as cultural object. EN: *The Return of the Time Machine*.

## Temporal structure
Wells machine + failed direct past / required future run-up; satirical pseudo-physics.
