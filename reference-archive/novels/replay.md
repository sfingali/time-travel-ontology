## Blurb
Radio journalist Jeff Winston dies of a heart attack in 1988 and awakens in his 1963 college body—again and again—replaying decades with memory intact until the loops shorten toward an unpredictable present.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
**Jeff Winston**, 43, dies in 1988 and wakes as an 18-year-old Emory University student in 1963 with intact memories of the next 25 years. He relives life differently each cycle—wealth via future knowledge, alternate careers and loves—yet always dies of a heart attack in 1988 and reboots, each time starting *later* (hours → days → weeks → years → decades later into the span).

In one replay he notices acclaimed 1974 film *Starsea* (Spielberg/Lucas involved) that never existed before; its unknown producer **Pamela Phillips** is another **replayer**. They confirm shared future-knowledge, fall in love, and discover their restart windows are shortening and desynchronizing (Pamela begins later than Jeff). Cryptic newspaper ads seeking other replayers yield a psychiatric-hospital inmate who knows futures *and* believes aliens force him to murder for entertainment.

They later go public with precise future announcements; government conscripts them as oracles, imprisoning them when they resist as geopolitics diverge violently from remembered history. Loops shrink until they repeatedly relive their original deaths—until Jeff survives the 1988 heart attack. Pamela has also survived; replaying was real. Epilogue: a Norwegian man wakes young in 1988 with memories through 2017—implying the phenomenon is neither unique to Jeff/Pamela nor limited to 1963–88.

World Fantasy Award (1988); Clarke shortlist. Foundational "life replay" text; often cited as precursor vibe to *Groundhog Day* without manufactured tech.
