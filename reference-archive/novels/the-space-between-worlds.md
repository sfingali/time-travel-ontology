## Blurb
Traverser Cara can only visit parallel Earths where her other selves are already dead—making rural "trash people" the empire's most valuable explorers—and the politics of Wiley City vs Ashtown cut across every world.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
Near-future multiverse travel is industrial. Rule: you may **traverse** to another Earth only if you are already dead there (else your presence is blocked / fatal). **Cara**, raised in wasteland **Ashtown**, is dead on most mapped worlds—hence elite **traverser** for corporate **Wiley City**. She harvests data/objects across worlds; her "watcher" **Dell** monitors.

Plot braids: Cara's family ties in Ashtown; entanglement with the wasteland's emperor across versions; class/romance tension with Dell; spiritual register of goddess **Nyame** felt in the literal **space between worlds**. Identity theme: same soul-pattern under different scarcity/privilege produces different Caras—some monsters, some kin. Imperial extraction metaphor (NYT: neoliberal imperialism) runs on the death-passport rule that makes the disposable most portable.

Awards: Compton Crook; Kitschies Golden Tentacle; Subjective Chaos Kind of Awards (2021); Locus Best First Novel finalist. Sequel: *Those Beyond the Wall* (2024).

## Temporal structure
Not time travel—**parallel worlds with technological interaction**. Death-passport is the hard constraint (diagram staple).

```
Earth-0 (Wiley/Ashtown) <--traverser--> Earth-n iff self dead on n
```
