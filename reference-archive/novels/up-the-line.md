## Blurb
Failed Harvard history student Jud Elliott becomes a Time Courier guiding tourists through Byzantium—until he falls for Pulcheria, his own multi-great-grandmother, and the Time Patrol closes in. Hugo/Nebula nominee; swinging-sixties TT farce.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
**2059:** **Jud Elliott II**, bored law clerk, joins the **Time Service** as Courier. "Up the line" = past; "down the line" = forward only to now-time 2059. After training he shepherds tourists through ancient and medieval **Byzantium/Constantinople**, especially Justinian's reign and the **Nika riots** of 532.

Problems: stupid tourists; greedy/unstable colleagues causing paradoxes he must patch without alerting the **Time Patrol**. Clever institutional details: cumulative tourists at the Crucifixion swell the crowd from dozens to thousands; Couriers must avoid eye contact with themselves leading other parties at the same event.

Jud falls for **"the marvelous transtemporal paradox called Pulcheria"**—his own multi-great-grandmother—succumbs to the past's lure, creates irreparable paradoxes, faces the Patrol. Sexually permissive late-60s Silverberg tone; Budrys complained the sex and Constantinople color inflate a novelette. Spawned 1990s YA spinoff series *Robert Silverberg's Time Tours* by other authors.
