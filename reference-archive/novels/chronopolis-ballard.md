## Blurb
Ballard short: in a future that banned clocks, prisoner Newman obsesses over measured time—and the time police who enforce a society without schedules.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
First pub. *New Worlds* June 1960; collected in *Chronopolis and Other Stories* (1971). Opens on **Newman** in prison; unfolds his fascination with time in a world where clocks are prohibited and **time police** regulate temporal order. Ballardian social-control riff: chronometry itself as contraband / ideology rather than machine tourism. Distinct from Barrington J. Bayley's 1974 novel *The Fall of Chronopolis* (time-war empire)—disambiguate if filing Bayley later. Influential on "time as institution" lists.

## Temporal structure
No traveler's machine; society-level suppression of clock time + enforcement agency.
