## Blurb
Grad student Bob Wilson meets two versions of himself, is shoved 30,000 years upwhen through a Time Gate, and discovers that chieftain "Diktor"—and the dictionary that makes rule possible—are all himself in a perfect causal loop.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)
1952: **Bob Wilson** locks himself in to finish a thesis arguing time travel is impossible. Interloper **"Joe"** (familiar-faced) arrives via a ~6-ft **Time Gate**, plies Bob with his own hidden liquor, urges him toward a go-getter's future kingdom. Fight with a third similar man knocks Bob through the Gate.

~30,000 CE: bearded **Diktor** greets him; docile post-High-Ones humanity; beautiful servants; Diktor wants Bob to fetch the man on the other side. Bob reenacts the room scene as Joe, then returns; Diktor issues a shopping list of 20th-c. goods. Annoyed, Bob later collects the list (bad check), mistreats a girlfriend, prank-calls past-self, finds his hat and a worn **English↔local dictionary** by the Gate controls. He jumps ten years earlier than Diktor's era to preempt him, builds chieftaincy under the title **Diktor** (local for "chief"), copies the falling-apart notebook into a twin, glimpses a High One (traumatic whitening of hair).

One day the Gate shows three selves in the familiar tableau; earliest Bob arrives—and present Bob *is* Diktor, the only Diktor there ever was. He must brief Bob to ensure his own past. Who first wrote the notebook remains the loop's unmoved mover problem. Classic single-timeline bootstrap; philosopher David Lewis cited it (with *All You Zombies*) as "perfectly consistent" time travel.
