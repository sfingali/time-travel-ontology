# 11.22.63 (2016)

## Short blurb
English teacher Jake Epping inherits dying friend Al’s diner portal to 21 October 1960 and a mission to stop JFK’s assassination. The past “pushes back”; love with Sadie Dunhill competes with the mission; success births a nightmarish present Jake must undo.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Lisbon, Maine, 2015/16: diner owner Al Templeton (Chris Cooper) shows Jake (James Franco) a pantry portal that always exits onto the same 1960 evening. Time in the present barely advances while years pass in the past; every trip resets the past to that default night (erasing prior sojourns). Dying of cancer after his own failed long stay, Al briefs Jake with research binders: stop Lee Harvey Oswald (and confirm conspiracy or lone gunman), having first tested the past by saving a family’s domestic-horror fate.

Jake as “James Amberson” settles in Jodie, Texas, teaches, and falls for librarian Sadie. The past resists—fires, accidents, destroyed notes, a burned boarding house. He infiltrates Oswald’s orbit, befriends Bill Turcotte, navigates Cuban/CIA threads, and repeatedly nearly loses Sadie to violence tied to his tampering. Despite warnings (including a yellow-card stranger tied to the portal’s “guardians”), Jake reaches Dealey Plaza on 22 November 1963 and stops the assassination.

Returning through the portal, he finds a dystopian present: endless war, ruined infrastructure, fractured society—King’s butterfly maximalism. Jake goes back once more, resets by *not* saving Kennedy (and navigating Sadie’s fate per the adaptation’s choices), and returns to a present only slightly healed—enough to seek a present-day Sadie analogue in some versions’ emotional cadence; the Hulu finale emphasizes accepting an imperfect timeline over forced utopia.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Portal rules dump** (Al): fixed 1960 landing; present-side minutes vs past years; full past reset on re-entry.
2. **Training change** (family-tragedy test) proves mutability *and* cost.
3. **Long infiltration** 1960–63: identity, teaching, Sadie romance.
4. **Past immune response** escalates as Jake nears Oswald/JFK.
5. **Dealey success** → dystopian present.
6. **Undo / accept** — thematic surrender to imperfect history.

Eight-episode Hulu miniseries; trims and reshapes novel subplots but keeps resistance + butterfly ending.

## Paradox analysis

- **Past as immune system:** Accidents cluster around chronal interference—King’s signature.
- **Reset-on-reentry:** Unlike Frequency’s live rewrite, re-using the portal wipes the wrought past—high stakes for leaving anyone behind.
- **Branching present:** Saving JFK yields worse macrohistory—anti-wish-fulfillment.
- **Love vs mission:** Sadie is the human cost accounting that pure “fix history” ignores.
- **Yellow card / steward:** Hint of a larger temporal bureaucracy (adapted lightly from the book’s mystery man).
