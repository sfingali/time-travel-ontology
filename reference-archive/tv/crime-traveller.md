# Crime Traveller (1997)

## Short blurb
Detective Jeff Slade and scientist Holly Turner use her father’s homemade time machine to revisit crime scenes a few hours earlier—bound by strict limits on changing the past—in an eight-episode BBC procedural.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Anthony Horowitz-created short series: Holly maintains a backyard/lab machine inherited from her father’s research; Jeff is the street-smart detective who partners with her. Episodes send them back to observe murders and robberies forming, gathering clues their present selves lacked. The machine’s rules—limited window, risks of paradox, inability to freely rewrite—turn each case into a puzzle about what *can* be altered versus what must be worked around. Comedy and danger mix as they dodge their past selves and corrupt colleagues. Only one series produced; cult niche among British TT procedurals.

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Crime breaks** in present.
2. **Machine spin-up** — short lookback.
3. **Covert observation / constrained intervention.**
4. **Return with actionable knowledge.**
5. **Arrest that respects (mostly) the rules.**

## Paradox analysis

- **Procedural constraint as drama:** Limits > omnipotence.
- **Closed-loop friendly:** Often they fulfill rather than smash causality.
- **DIY machine aesthetic:** Anti-TEC; personal/scientific oddity.
- **Horowitz puzzle DNA:** Mystery plotting with TT garnish.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

BBC One; 8 episodes. Michael French post-*EastEnders*. Rarely repeated; DVD/streaming spotty. Spiritual cousin to *Early Edition* and *Deja Vu*’s investigative lookback.
