# DC's Legends of Tomorrow (2016–2022)

## Short blurb
A ragtag team aboard the timeship *Waverider* fixes (and causes) historical anachronisms across the Arrowverse. Tone shifts from grim time-war to found-family absurdist comedy while still running aberration-of-the-week and big temporal myth arcs (Legion of Doom, Time Bureau, Loom of Fate, aliens, goddamn Beebo).

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Rip Hunter recruits heroes/villains (Sara Lance, Snart, Rory, Firestorm, Atom, Hawks) to stop Vandal Savage across centuries. Early seasons play time travel semi-seriously—fixed points, aberrations, Time Masters. The team repeatedly breaks history, fights their evil doppelgangers, and topples the Time Masters’ control.

Later seasons lean into comedy: Time Bureau bureaucracy; magical-seasons with John Constantine; a unicorn; puppets; the twinning of Sara’s leadership with found-family ethos. Major arcs include Legion of Doom (reverse-engineered Spear of Destiny reality rewrite), Mallus, Encores, the Loom of Fate (Clotho), alien invasion via TV-land, and final-season turns toward ending the team’s endless obligation. Cameos braid Flash/Arrow/Supergirl crossovers (*Crisis on Infinite Earths* remaps the multiverse around them).

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Savage hunt** — classic timeship pilot premise.
2. **Time Masters fall** — no more central traffic control.
3. **Legion / Spear** — reality rewrite MacGuffin.
4. **Bureau era** — workplace comedy + aberrations.
5. **Magic & Loom** — fate vs free will literalized.
6. **Crisis adjacent** — multiverse reboot impact.
7. **Sunset the mission** — series close on team choice.

## Paradox analysis

- **Aberration patchwork:** Monster-of-week as timeline symptom.
- **Soft fixed points:** Rules flex for jokes then harden for finales.
- **Spear / Loom:** Explicit destiny-manipulation artifacts.
- **Team as aberration:** Show admits protagonists destabilize history by existing—leaned into as theme.
- **Arrowverse braid:** Crossovers import Flashpoint-style reboot logic.

## Endings / production notes

The CW; seven seasons. Became the “weird sitcom with time travel” of the Berlanti-verse. Strong queer representation via Sara Lance et al.
