# The King: Eternal Monarch (더 킹: 영원의 군주, 2020)

> **DIAGRAM FLAG:** Dual-world cast maps (Corea Empire ↔ ROK) and flute-half logistics invite charts; fan doppelgänger tables common.

## Short blurb
Emperor Lee Gon of the Kingdom of Corea crosses a bamboo-forest door opened by the broken Manpasikjeok flute into a Republic of Korea where detective Jeong Tae-eul matches an ID card that once saved his child self—while half-uncle Lee Lim hops worlds and times toward immortality.

## Detailed plot summary
(Source: English Wikipedia.) 16-episode SBS/Netflix romantic fantasy (Apr–Jun 2020), budget reportedly >₩30 billion. As a child, Lee Gon survives his father’s assassination by Lee Lim when an unknown adult bearing Tae-eul’s ID intervenes; the Four Tiger Sword splits the mythical flute. Lee Lim flees with one half into a parallel Republic of Korea, kills his counterpart, and later learns the reunited flute enables multi-reality access and time travel. Adult Lee Gon—mathematician-emperor—seeks the other half and meets Inspector Jeong Tae-eul of Jongno’s Violent Crimes Squad; her Corean counterpart is terminal criminal Luna. Dual casting spans Jo Yeong / Jo Eun-sup (Woo Do-hwan), Kang Shin-jae / Kang Hyeon-min, and PM Koo Seo-ryeong / Koo Eun-ah. A mysterious “yoyo boy” appears to regulate the parallel universes. Lee Lim assembles cross-world armies; Lee Gon and Tae-eul close the portal conflict across palace intrigue and Seoul police work. Mixed domestic ratings vs strong Netflix Asia performance; controversies over Japanese-temple-like set design and gender-stereotype advisories.

## Timeline/structure notes
- Two coexisting geographies: Kingdom of Corea (surviving monarchy) vs Republic of Korea.
- Flute halves = portal keys; full flute escalates to time/multiplicity.
- Childhood ID-card rescue is a closed-ish loop completed when adult Tae-eul’s card reaches young Gon.
