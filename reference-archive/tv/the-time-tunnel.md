# The Time Tunnel (1966–1967)

## Short blurb
Project Tic-Toc scientists Tony Newman and Doug Phillips are lost inside America’s vast underground time tunnel, materializing weekly at historical crises—Titanic, Little Bighorn, Pearl Harbor-adjacent flashpoints—while the control-room team races to lock on and pull them home.

## Detailed plot summary (spoilers)

Irwin Allen adventure SF: enormous circular Tunnel set, banks of 1960s computers, generals and scientists watching Tony & Doug tumble from era to era. Episodes are historical tourism with guest peril: they warn, fight, or survive famous disasters, rarely permanently altering textbook outcomes. Cold open often dumps them mid-catastrophe; act breaks cut back to Tic-Toc’s frustrated retrieval attempts. Season ends without permanent rescue—template for later “lost in time” shows. Guest historical figures and stock-footage spectacle define the Allen house style (*Voyage to the Bottom of the Sea*, *Lost in Space*).

## Beat-by-beat temporal structure

1. **Tunnel accident** — travelers unmoored.
2. **Era drop** into documented crisis.
3. **Local adventure** + moral choice.
4. **Control-room lock-on attempt.**
5. **Slip to next cliffhanger** before retrieval.

## Paradox analysis

- **Anthology history:** Past as theme park with danger.
- **Soft closed loops:** Famous events usually proceed; heroes are witnesses/helpers.
- **Retrieval as holy grail:** Series promise never fully paid in-run.
- **Template status:** Direct ancestor of *Voyagers!* and DNA in *Quantum Leap* / *Timeless*.

## Endings / cuts / production notes

ABC; 30 episodes. Iconic Tunnel visual. Fox/Irwin Allen library revivals discussed for decades. Educational veneer over adventure.
